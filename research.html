Research

Chaotic systems: how to forecast them, and how to understand what the model learned.

My work sits between fluid mechanics, nonlinear dynamics, machine learning and geometric theory. The common aim is to make complicated systems predictable where they can be, and intelligible where they cannot, without simplifying away their real structure.

Core themes

Turbulence, chaos, forecasting, latent causality, dissipation and geometric transport.

Typical tools

DNS, transformers and attention, β-VAEs, reduced-order models, SHAP and SURD, variational arguments.

Style of work

Rigorous and patient. A model is finished when it can explain itself, not when the loss stops falling.

Research areas

Main directions.

Transformers · robustness

Forecasting chaotic systems

Model classes that stay robust on nonlinear dynamics and keep long-term statistics right, instead of optimising a narrow one-step target. Easy Attention is the reference result.

ROMs · closures · generative models

Surrogate and reduced-order models

Cheap models that stand in for expensive simulations: deep-learning closures for algebraic turbulence surrogates, and hybrid generative ROMs of the minimal flow unit.

β-VAE · SURD · SHAP

Interpretable latent dynamics

Compression is only useful when latent coordinates map back to coherent structures, causal organisation and the physical geometry of the flow. X-CAL does exactly that.

Wall turbulence · asymptotics

Geometric turbulence

Can dissipation, wall laws and intermittency be derived from geometric, entropic and fixed-point principles, instead of being introduced only through empirical closure?

Pressure · geometry · fields

Unified flow programmes

Connecting pressure, information geometry, viscoelastic analogies and large-scale formulations under one mathematical vocabulary.

Industry · HPC

Applied work

A Marie Skłodowska-Curie secondment at Microflown Technologies (acoustic sensing), and auto-tuned multi-GPU simulation for urban air mobility.

Beyond fluids

Where the same methods apply.

Turbulence is the hardest test case I know. The tools built for it carry over to any system where small errors grow fast and data is expensive.

Engineering and energy

Surrogates that replace slow simulations inside design and control loops.

Chaotic time series

Forecasts that report how far ahead they can be trusted, not just a point estimate.

Trustworthy AI

Explanations tied to the structure of the system, so an expert can check them.

Methods

How the work gets done.

Fluid mechanics

Wall-bounded turbulence, shear flows, transport, coherent structures and asymptotic arguments.

Machine learning

Representation learning, attention mechanisms, explainability tools and reduced-complexity models.

Mathematics

Geometric interpretation, variational reasoning, nonlinear dynamics and structure-first formulation.

Computation

DNS and numerical experiments on GPUs, and workflows that move between theory and data.

Current themes

Open questions.

  • Geometric turbulence

    Universal wall structure from stochastic and geometric constraints

    Whether the Kolmogorov scale and the law of the wall can be tied to one deeper principle of path uncertainty and fixed-point structure.

  • Interpretable AI

    Latent causality with physical localisation

    Whether causal relations inferred in latent space can be mapped back reliably to wake structures, wall interactions and flow organisation.

  • Nonlinear prediction

    Reduced-complexity attention for chaotic systems

    What simpler attention mechanisms reveal about invariance, robustness and temporal structure.
